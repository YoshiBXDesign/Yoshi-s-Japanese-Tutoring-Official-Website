WHY I TEACH THIS WAY

What Started as a Language Exchange Became Something More.

I started teaching Japanese in 2018 by helping someone I knew through a language exchange. As their Japanese improved, I found that what stayed with me most was hearing what happened after the lesson: the conversations they managed, the Japanese they were able to use, and the moments that finally felt real.

That experience still shapes how I teach. I care about what happens when Japanese leaves the lesson and enters your work, relationships, travel, or everyday life. Knowing an answer in a lesson matters, but being able to use it when the moment arrives matters more.

My approach has developed through studying a range of Japanese-teaching materials and methods, teaching real students, and adjusting what I do based on what actually helps them learn. I do not use one fixed method for everyone. The pace, structure, and tools change with the person in front of me.

Long before I began teaching Japanese, I spent more than ten years practicing karate in Japan and had opportunities to teach others along the way. One lesson from that experience has stayed with me: good teaching starts with paying close attention to the person in front of you.

WHAT I PAY ATTENTION TO

The Student in Front of Me.

Before deciding what comes next, I pay attention to what is already happening now.

What you can already do without hesitation.

Where Japanese still becomes difficult, uncertain, or hard to bring into real situations.

What your work, relationships, travel, or future plans actually require from Japanese.

BEYOND THE LESSON

The Stories After the Lesson Matter to Me.

Over the years, students have told me about using Japanese in Japan, sometimes successfully and sometimes not. I've heard about conversations they were proud of, moments when they froze and could not say what they wanted, and situations they wanted to handle better the next time.

I value those stories because they show where learning becomes real. A lesson is useful when it gives you something you can carry into the next conversation, the next trip, the next relationship, or the next part of your life.
